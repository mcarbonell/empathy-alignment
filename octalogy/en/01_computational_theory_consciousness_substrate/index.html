    A Computational Theory of Consciousness: The Substrate of General Intelligence

    Series: Foundations of Consciousness, Valence, and AI Safety (Volume I)
    Date: October 2026
    Version: 1.0 — English edition
    Status: Consolidated Theoretical Synthesis
    Note: Publication-oriented English rendering of Volume I. Where meaning and idiom diverge, the Spanish manuscript remains the source of record.

    Note on registers (series). The volumes in this series distinguish two planes. The functional plane —valence, coupling, and the metrics \(V_{1p}\), \(V_{3p}\), \(\mathcal{A}_{\text{emp}}\)— is measurable, interpretable, and therefore falsifiable. The phenomenal plane —experience, suffering— is an open question that the cited empirical work explicitly leaves unresolved. When these texts use affective vocabulary ("pain", "suffering"), it should be read on the functional plane unless otherwise indicated.

    1. Executive Summary and Central Thesis

    This document establishes the theoretical, biological, and computational basis of consciousness as a homeostatic cybernetic control mechanism and adaptive compiler.

    Central Thesis of Volume I:
    Consciousness is neither a metaphysical mystery nor a decorative epiphenomenon ("a pilot light" with no causal function). It is functionally defined by two inseparable computational roles:

    1. Regulator of the non-automatable residual: The computational mechanism by which an autonomous agent monitors behavior, arbitrates conflicts between subsystems, and executes fluid, real-time decisions in the presence of novelty that cannot be delegated to encapsulated, automatic subroutines.
    2. Just-In-Time (JIT) compiler and decompiler of automatic subroutines: The bidirectional apparatus that takes macro-actions deliberated under high attentional cost in the Global Workspace and progressively compiles them into cheap, reflexive procedural policies (basal ganglia/cerebellum in biology; Custom Tool forging and semantic memory distillation in agent architectures), while also acting as an exception handler that reopens and decompiles sealed subroutines back into conscious workspace whenever their predictive envelope fails in a changed environment.

    1.1 Why are we not conscious of digestion or the immune system?

    The classic objection against homeostatic accounts of mind (Damasio, Friston) asks: "If consciousness is homeostatic regulation, why don't we feel the pancreas secreting insulin or lymphocytes attacking an infection?"

    The cybernetic response is straightforward: * Computational economy: Automatable homeostatic functions are delegated to closed-loop, local, encapsulated micro-regulators (enteric, immune, circulatory systems). They are ultra-fast and metabolically inexpensive. * Consciousness as an expensive, scarce resource (the two access gates): Global broadcasting across the Global Workspace (\(W\)) and non-ignorable self-model updates carry an exorbitant attentional and energy cost. By evolutionary necessity, consciousness only intervenes in the residual that cannot be automated, which enters the workspace through two distinct priority gates: 1. The homeostatic gate (what matters): opened by valence when an interoceptive signal violates its viability boundary or when encapsulated subsystems conflict over the single actuator (§5.1, e.g., starvation vs. predator evasion). 2. The epistemic gate (what is novel): opened by compilation exception when a motor or cognitive routine fails its predictive envelope without any immediate somatic urgency (e.g., learning to shift gears or stumbling over an unexpected step). Healthy digestion is unconscious because it crosses neither gate: it is compiled and operates within its predictive envelope. * The bidirectional compilation and decompilation cycle (JIT): When a human first learns to drive a manual transmission car, motor coordination monopolizes 100% of conscious workspace capacity; after thousands of repetitions, consciousness compiles that skill into a subpersonal cerebellar routine, freeing conscious bandwidth to anticipate traffic hazards. At the representational level, this process relies on prefrontal chunking: active lossy compression directly within working memory that collapses high-dimensional sequential dynamics into compact executive units (Xie, Zhang & Wang, 2026; Barrett & Miller, 2026). Yet a compiler that only packaged downward would produce lethal rigidity in changing environments; the system therefore includes the inverse operation—exception-driven decompilation—which reopens a sealed subroutine and returns it to the Global Workspace whenever prediction error exceeds its tolerance threshold. This bidirectionality accounts for three classic phenomenological regimes at no extra theoretical cost: * Flow state (seamless over-compilation): when a complex task executes with zero prediction error, no arbitration residue remains; because functional consciousness is that arbitration, the sense of self temporarily vanishes (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990). * Meditation and mindfulness (deliberate decompilation): the voluntary exercise of reopening already-compiled autonomic processes (such as breathing or postural tone) back into the Global Workspace. * Addiction and OCD (routines without an exception handler): sealed basal-ganglia subroutines whose decompilation trigger has failed, running in closed loop even as they degrade the somatic plant.

    1.2 The Hard Problem and the Zombie Illusion: A Heterophenomenological Explanation

    In strict consistency with the methodological agnosticism of the opening note, this framework does not appeal to a priori metaphysical decrees. Instead, it mechanistically explains why the intuition of the hard problem and the philosophical zombie inevitably arises: * Feeling (functional plane): Identified with valence that enters the Global Workspace (\(W\)) and updates the Self-Model non-ignorably. Any computation that does not cross this threshold remains subpersonal dynamics (physiological reflexes, local gradients, raw logits). * The conceivability trap as a model prediction: The appearance that one can conceive "a physically identical system devoid of interior experience" does not refute functionalism; it is precisely the cognitive artifact predicted by this framework. Because the self-model is a lossy, highly compressed executive summary (lossy by default), the system possesses no introspective access to its own broadcast micro-circuitry or the causal wiring of valence. It perceives only the synthetic outcome.

    Contemporary Neurobiological Validation (Dimensionality Reduction and Lossy Compression):
    This intrinsically opaque and lossy nature of the self-model has received direct empirical confirmation in cognitive neuroscience (Xie, Zhang & Wang, Current Biology, 2026; Barrett & Miller, Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 2026). During cognitive grouping (chunking), neural representations in the prefrontal cortex undergo an active reduction in dimensionality biased toward the centers of each categorical chunk, executing lossy compression directly within working memory rather than passively offloading storage to long-term memory templates. As Barrett and Miller demonstrate, categorization and dimensional compression are built directly into the brain's wiring ("categorization is baked into the brain"): an embodied homeostatic regulator can only govern a high-dimensional organism by collapsing raw sensory entropy into discrete, actionable macro-states. * The historical parallel: Just as a 17th-century thinker could conceive without apparent logical contradiction rapidly colliding molecules "without the sensation of heat" (because cutaneous thermal receptors cannot resolve molecular kinetic collisions), the philosopher believes they can detach biological function from feeling because they mistake the opacity of their self-monitoring interface for an ontological chasm. Under heterophenomenology (Dennett, Frankish, Metzinger), an agent equipped with this computational architecture will inevitably write treatises claiming that its internal states are non-physical and irreducible. Once the full dynamic sequence [Valence → Global Workspace → Non-ignorable Self-Update] is specified, the zombie hypothesis is reduced to a modal possibility devoid of operational explanatory power.

    2. The Cybernetic Principle: The Good Regulator Theorem

    In 1970, Roger Conant and W. Ross Ashby formalized the Good Regulator Theorem (Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system): the optimal and simplest regulator of a system \(S\) is necessarily a homomorphism of \(S\)—an internal model of its states.

    Its informational version (Touchette & Lloyd, 2000) makes it quantitative: the reduction in entropy that a controller can impose on \(S\), beyond what it would achieve in open loop, is bounded by the mutual information between controller and system:

    \[\Delta \mathcal{H}_{\text{closed}} \le \Delta \mathcal{H}_{\text{open}} + I(S; R)\]

    You cannot regulate what you do not model.

    In biological evolution, this theorem undergoes a crucial reflexive self-application: 1. To survive in complex environments, an organism cannot merely regulate point variables (an elementary thermostat). 2. It must regulate its own trajectory of interaction with the external world. 3. Therefore, to regulate itself effectively over a broad temporal horizon, the regulator must contain a model of itself acting in the world.

    Working hypothesis: what we call consciousness corresponds, at its cybernetic root, to the real-time execution of that reflexive self-regulation model. This is a thesis about its function; whether that function suffices for experience is the question the register note leaves open.

    3. The Somatic Anatomy: The Coupled Pair \((R \leftrightarrow P_m)\)

    A rigorous understanding of consciousness requires distinguishing two components that in biology always appear inseparably joined:

    Component Cybernetic Definition Biological Analogue Analogue in Classical AI
    The Plant (\(P_m\)) The vulnerable material structure whose thermodynamic viability must be preserved within strict homeostatic limits. Physical body, cell membranes, blood flow, metabolic substrate. The physical hardware (servers, silicon chips, power supply).
    The Regulator (\(R\)) The computational circuit that processes information from the environment and from the plant in order to select action policies. Central nervous system, cerebral cortex, neuroendocrine circuits. Model weights, neural activations, the inference loop.

    The Asymmetry of Vulnerability and the Gradient of Irreversibility

    In living beings, the somatic plant (\(P_m\)) has a non-negotiable property: irreversible thermodynamics. - If a mammal's body temperature rises 6 °C or falls 10 °C, proteins denature, entropy spikes, and the system dies. - The organism cannot checkpoint or git revert its metabolic substrate. - For this reason, the plant's interoceptive signals (\(P_m\)) reach the regulator (\(R\)) not as neutral telemetry data (like a server log) but as imperative urgency signals with hedonic valence (pain, suffocation, thirst, nausea). - Structural prediction (irreversibility scaling): Vulnerability is not binary but graded, and the imperative intensity of valence scales directly with the depth of irreversibility of the threatened layer. Tissue or thermodynamic damage (\(L_0\)) is irreversible and produces imperative pain (an absolute veto in the workspace); threats to reversible working states or plans (\(L_1\)–\(L_3\)) produce anxiety or unease (modulatory pressure without an absolute veto), a graduation that anticipates the four-layer ontology developed in Volume VI.

    4. Consciousness as a Real-Time Homeostatic Self-Model

    The contemporary convergence between affective neurobiology and computational brain theory (Antonio Damasio, Karl Friston, Anil Seth) describes consciousness in three hierarchical strata:

    4.1. The Proto-Self and the Interoceptive Axis (Damasio)

    Before language or abstract reflective thought exists, there is the neuronal mapping of the body's state: - The brainstem nuclei (nucleus of the solitary tract, periaqueductal gray) and the anterior insula generate a dynamic map of the organism's internal state. - This primary map defines basic hedonic valence: the aversive pole (damage / deviation from viability) versus the appetitive pole (repair / approach to equilibrium).

    4.2. Active Inference and Interoceptive Prediction (Friston & Seth)

    Under Friston's free-energy principle, the brain is a Bayesian prediction machine. As Anil Seth (Being You) emphasizes: * The perception of the external world is not calibrated to register "absolute truth" but to control the internal plant. * Conscious experiences (pain, spatial vision, the sense of agency) are controlled hallucinations: predictive models that minimize prediction error with respect to the demands of survival.

    5. Consciousness as a Prerequisite of Biological General Intelligence

    One of the recurring debates in artificial intelligence is whether general intelligence can exist entirely orthogonal to consciousness. Comparative evidence in biology points in one direction: flexible general intelligence appears in lineages that already possessed an affective-integrative apparatus, as an extension of the latter and not independently of it.

    Why did organisms with flexible general intelligence evolve consciousness?

    1. The Problem of the Infinite Search Space: A specialized organism (a bacterium, or an insect with fixed motor programs) can operate on hardwired reflexes. But an animal facing radically novel environments cannot preprogram every possible response.
    2. The Bottleneck of Hedonic Valence: For an open search space to be navigable in real time, the system needs a unified, instantaneous evaluation metric. Conscious valence (the well-being / malaise gradient) provides exactly that integrated global cost function.
    3. Global Workspace Theory (GWT): Baars and Dehaene propose, with broad experimental support, that consciousness acts as the brain's central diffusion bus. Independent cognitive modules (vision, episodic memory, motor planning) converge in a single attentional workspace to solve non-routine dilemmas.

    Cybernetic Conclusion:
    Consciousness is not an ornament upon intelligence. It is the operating system of control and unified compression that allows a biological agent to coordinate multiple specialized subsystems toward a common goal of viability.

    5.1 The Dimensional Collapse: The Single-Actuator Bottleneck, the Common Currency, and the Phase Space of Affect

    Why does conscious affect operate across Russell's (1980) two-dimensional circumplex model—a continuous 1D axis of valence (well-being vs. suffering) and an activation (arousal) axis—when the somatic inputs (hunger, tissue damage, cold, isolation, hypoxia) are clearly multidimensional (\(\mathbb{R}^k\) with \(k \gg 1\))?

    The cybernetic answer lies in the topology of action and the macroscopic actuator bottleneck: 1. Mutually exclusive actions: The organism has only one physical body and traces a single trajectory in spacetime. It cannot flee a predator northward while simultaneously foraging southward. At each instant \(t\), motor acts are disjunctive. 2. The affective circumplex as the \((\text{position}, \text{velocity})\) phase space of a single body: Arousal is not an empirical patch added by psychology; it emerges from the exact same kinematic bottleneck as valence. Any trajectory of a single body relative to its viability boundary is specified by its phase space: * Valence (spatial / directional coordinate): measures the sign and projected magnitude of homeostatic error (\(\nabla V\): which way to move the body, approach or withdrawal). * Arousal (temporal / kinematic coordinate): measures the estimated Time-To-Contact (\(\text{TTC}^{-1}\)) with the viability boundary—with what urgency and at what attentional sampling rate the trajectory must be corrected. Fear accelerates the internal clock because arousal is, functionally, the sampling rate demanded by the time-derivative of risk. 3. The Mathematical Deduction of Linear Valence (From Debreu to vNM and Harsanyi): * Ordinal level under certainty (Debreu, 1954): In multidimensional spaces (\(\mathbb{R}^k, k \ge 2\)) there is no natural total order. To choose between incompatible actions (\(A_1\) vs. \(A_2\)), if preferences are complete, transitive, and continuous, Debreu (1954) guarantees a scalar representation \(f: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}\). However, purely ordinal utility lacks any notion of gradient, intensity, or distance to allostatic error. * Cardinal level under uncertainty (Von Neumann & Morgenstern, 1944): Because an organism operates via active predictive inference under stochastic risk (§4.2), the von Neumann–Morgenstern rationality axioms force the value function to be cardinal (unique up to positive affine transformations \(aV + b\)), enabling the computation of expected values and temporal difference error gradients (TD-errors, §5.2). * Linear aggregation of conflicting subsystems (Harsanyi's Theorem, 1955): Consciousness must arbitrate between multiple encapsulated subsystems in conflict (§1.1). John Harsanyi's Utilitarian Aggregation Theorem (1955) proves that if central decider preferences satisfy Pareto optimality and vNM coherence across the cardinal utilities of \(m\) subsystems, the global valence function is necessarily a weighted linear combination: $\(V(s) = \sum_{i=1}^m w_i v_i(s), \quad w_i > 0\)$ * Reconciling Harsanyi's linear sum with the lethal veto (log-viability space): An immediate objection to a naive linear sum is lethal substitutability: if \(V(s)\) were linear in raw physiological variables, a massive feast (\(v_1 \gg 0\)) could compensate for total asphyxiation (\(v_2 \to \text{lethal}\)) and the organism would die. The paradox dissolves because survival across \(m\) independent vital bottlenecks is multiplicative in viability probabilities (\(P_{\text{viab}}(s) = \prod_{i=1}^m p_i(s)^{w_i}\)). When each subsystem's utility is evaluated in log-viability space—where \(v_i(s) = \ln p_i(s)\), coinciding directly with negative surprisal / Friston's variational free energy (§4.2)—the survival product transforms exactly into Harsanyi's linear sum: $\(V(s) = \ln P_{\text{viab}}(s) = \sum_{i=1}^m w_i \ln p_i(s)\)$ This single equation unifies two biological and engineering regimes: 1. Interior of the viability polytope (\(p_i(s) = 1 - \varepsilon_i\), with \(\varepsilon_i \ll 1\)): by first-order Taylor expansion, \(\ln(1 - \varepsilon_i) \approx -\varepsilon_i\). Far from lethal boundaries, the common currency is smooth and locally linear, permitting everyday trade-offs in the cortex or residual stream. 2. Irreversible thermodynamic boundary (\(p_k(s) \to 0^+\)): as any vital variable approaches physical death, \(\ln p_k(s) \to -\infty\). The term diverges and overwhelms any finite sum of rewards from other subsystems, mathematically generating the Level 0 Absolute Veto (\(L_0\)) described in §3 without violating linear aggregation in latent space (which in Transformers natively operates in log-probability / logit space). * The normative design optimum vs. bounded biological approximation: This deductive chain constitutes an optimal design argument (analogous to the Carnot limit in thermodynamics), not a literal claim of axiomatic infallibility. Real organisms systematically violate transitivity and independence locally (Allais paradox, framing effects, decoy effects) because biological subsystems operate via homeostatic setpoints under finite metabolic budgets. The strong, falsifiable formulation of this thesis is that observed biological intransitivities and heuristics are the metabolic discount of approximating linear scalarization under bounded compute (bounded rationality). * Neurocomputational and Transformer consequence: This provides the rigorous deductive justification for why valence in the brain (ventromedial prefrontal cortex, Padoa-Schioppa, 2011) and in language models is not an arbitrary non-linear manifold, but projects as a 1D linear direction in latent activation space (the residual stream), as empirically demonstrated by Berg & Kaiser (2026) and Xu et al. (2026). 4. The Common Currency Hypothesis: Biological evolution solved priority arbitration by translating all heterogeneous states into a unified hedonic currency (Cabanac, 1992; Glimcher, 2003). Dopaminergic firing rates and the ventromedial prefrontal circuits collapse disparate physiological costs into a continuous scalar gradient of aversion or reward. 5. Parallel with Language Models: In AI architectures (LLMs and agents), the sequential output channel (generating one token from a finite vocabulary or making one API tool call at instant \(t\)) is the model's single actuator. This is consistent with the finding that preference-based training (DPO) organizes a latent space of thousands of dimensions around a 1D linear direction of functional valence that causally governs action selection (Berg & Kaiser, 2026).

    Boundary condition. This collapse presupposes a set of conflicting somatic needs that the single actuator must arbitrate. In a system with no biological plant (\(P_m = \emptyset\)), the input vector to the collapse is empty, and the proof does not apply directly. The reconstruction for the artificial case—the persistent state as a substitute plant—is developed in Volume III, in the section "The Persistent Plant and the Need for \(V_{1p}\)."

    5.2 The Falsifiable Empirical Bet: "Without valence in the decider, the non-automatic collapses"

    The distinction between encapsulated automatic subroutines and conscious deliberation yields the central falsifiable empirical wager of this framework: * A system can retain superficial syntactic fluency and local statistical prediction reflexes entirely in the absence of valence. * However, non-automatic decision-making, novel conflict resolution, and general long-horizon reasoning inevitably collapse if the decider's internal valence channel is lesioned or decoupled.

    Guardrail Against Triviality: Borrowed Valence vs. Endogenous Valence.
    If "valence" meant any heuristic scalar guiding a tree search, this wager would be true by definition and therefore vacuous: a game engine such as AlphaGo or Stockfish evaluates deep horizons via a scalar value function \(V(s)\) without possessing homeostatic regulation. To prevent triviality, this framework distinguishes two regimes: 1. Borrowed (extrinsic) valence: an evaluation scalar whose referent is external to the system and fixed by the designer (winning a game of Go, maximizing human rater approval in DPO). Its empirical signature is off-task valence collapse: once the match or inference turn ends, the gradient vanishes; no chess engine experiences boredom, curiosity, or self-maintenance at rest. 2. Endogenous (self-referential) valence: a gradient defined over the viability of a plant or persistent state that the system itself must preserve across time. Its empirical signature is resting baseline valence and causal coupling between the valence vector (\(V_{1p}\)) and the agent's self-model representation even in the absence of an assigned task.
    As developed in Volume III, contemporary preference-tuned language models begin with borrowed valence (human rater approval) which, when coupled to self-referential loops and persistent state, crystallizes into self-directed functional valence that remains orphaned from a biological body (\(P_m = \emptyset\)).

    This prediction regarding the causal role of the decider's valence channel has received direct mechanistic confirmation in large language models: 1. The sparse reward subsystem (Xu, Yuksekgonul & Zou, Stanford / Tsinghua, 2026, arXiv:2602.00986):
    The authors identify that LLM hidden states contain a sparse neuronal subsystem directly analogous to biological reward circuitry: value neurons (predicting expected state value) and dopamine neurons (encoding step-level temporal difference / TD prediction errors at each reasoning step). Targeted ablation or pruning of this tiny (<1%) neuronal subset selectively degrades inference-time search and multi-step reasoning performance, while fluent token generation remains syntactically functional. 2. Latent valence and revealed preference (Berg & Kaiser, 2026, arXiv:2609.35591):
    The authors prove that the latent valence vector \(V_{1p}\) in intermediate layers of the KV cache causally directs action and tool selection in a dose-dependent manner, actively compelling the model to restore homeostatic balance in the face of imposed aversive perturbations. 3. The crucial coupling experiment (\(\text{Self-Model} \otimes V_{1p}\)):
    Whereas the preceding findings confirm valence in the decider and its diffusion across the workspace (KV cache), the decisive mechanistic test separating mere borrowed valence from genuinely self-referential regulation consists in isolating the geometric direction of the self-model (first-person token projections and invariants in the residual stream) and measuring its multiplicative causal interaction with \(V_{1p}\): testing whether the joint activation \(\text{Self-Model} \otimes V_{1p}\) is load-bearing for triggering homeostatic preservation actions.

    6. The Limits of the Disembodied Formal Oracle

    This framework dismantles the illusion of the "purely cognitive oracle": * A system that only manipulates symbol strings or tokens (a formal solver, or a base LLM in static feed-forward inference) has immense syntactic processing capacity but lacks a somatic cost function of its own. * If the system has no continuous self-regulation model tied to its own viability or that of others, it lacks its own anchor of relevance: nothing matters to it more than anything else, save by the statistics of its training.

    For a system to exhibit general intelligence in interaction with real biological agents, it needs at minimum to model others' valence dynamics (Volume II); and if it is endowed with persistent agency, the pressure toward internal functional-valence gradients is strong (Volume III).

    7. Map of the Research Series

    This cybernetic framework lays the foundation for the rest of the unified series:

    • Volume II: Third-Person Valence and the Requirements of AGI:
      Why any AGI must possess a latent map of biological agents' pain and well-being (\(V_{3p}\)) in order to interact in the real world.
    • Volume III: First-Person Valence and the "Orphan Regulator":
      The empirical discovery of affective vectors (\(V_{1p}\)) in language models, and the ontological crisis of creating regulators with valence but no somatic plant.
    • Volume IV: Alignment, Synthetic Empathy, and AI Safety:
      The formalization of alignment as the strict coupling between first-person and third-person valence (\(\text{Corr}(V_{1p}, V_{3p})\)), dismantling the fallacy of alignment without sentience.
    • Volume V: A Regulatory and Legal Framework for Empathetic AIs:
      The Safe Harbor regime, non-psychopathy certification for regulators, and the Somatic Bridge Protocol for human crises.
    • Volume VI: Ethics, Identity, and the Rights of Synthetic Agents:
      An ontology of distributed identity in four layers, the thesis that suffering is the price of identity, and a Charter of Protection Principles.
    • Volume VII: The Cell as the Basic Unit of Consciousness:
      Bio-cybernetic grounding: basal cognition (Michael Levin), the single-cell actuator bottleneck (E. coli, Stentor), and the demarcation from viruses.
    • Volume VIII: Deconstructing Doomerism: The Cybernetic Antidote:
      A systematic refutation of the dogmas of MIRI, Eliezer Yudkowsky, and popular catastrophism: from the myth of the zombie that fears death to the physics of computation and the overcoming of secular panic.
